A counterexample to the Shannon conjecture following Eq. (69) in Tarabunga et al., Section V.C

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang ask whether removing a quantum state’s signs and phases reliably gives a lower estimate of measurement uncertainty across a particular family of bases. Their answer is no: a three-qubit counterexample defeats the proposed rule, even for strictly positive input amplitudes. They also identify surviving results and a weaker, valid replacement.

A quantum state assigns complex amplitudes to binary strings, with squared magnitudes summing to one. Measurement in an orthonormal basis produces probabilities; Shannon entropy measures their uncertainty. The bases here, called stabilizer bases, combine binary Fourier transforms with restricted linear and quadratic phases. A binary support matrix specifies affine blocks: translates of a binary linear subspace. Tarabunga, Frau, Haug, Tirrito and Piroli conjectured that, fixing this matrix, taking absolute amplitudes and zero basis phases gives an entropy lower bound for every permitted phase choice. Such bounds let an optimization search discard branches that cannot improve its current best answer. They proved the corresponding inequality for integer Rényi indices above one, but only conjectured the Shannon extension.

Ma and Zhang take three qubits, with all binary strings forming one block. In order 000 through 111, their normalized amplitudes are (1,0,2,−1,1,0,0,1)/√8. In the zero-phase basis, these give four equiprobable outcomes, hence entropy log 4. Taking absolute amplitudes instead gives one outcome of probability 9/16 and seven of probability 1/16. Its entropy, log 16 − (9/8)log 3, is strictly larger; logarithms are natural. Interference explains the failure: the negative amplitude creates cancellations concentrating the Fourier outcomes. One admissible basis below the proposed bound suffices to disprove it, without finding a global optimum.

The input need not contain a negative sign. For the absolute-amplitude state, an allowed quadratic basis phase restores the required signs on its occupied strings, reproducing the contradiction. Replacing zero amplitudes by sufficiently small positive numbers and renormalizing preserves the gap by continuity. For the unperturbed absolute-amplitude state, the authors determine the fixed-support Shannon minimum as log 4, not a minimum over all support matrices. They also clarify a phase convention: real amplitudes allow minimization over real bases, but eliminating imaginary linear phases can require adjusting the quadratic phase. Deleting the former while holding the latter fixed is not generally valid under their convention.

Rényi entropies vary how strongly large probabilities matter; index one gives Shannon entropy, and index zero gives the logarithm of support size. For these distributions, the proposed inequality fails exactly from zero up to, but excluding, a threshold above one, approximately 1.262623. The earlier integer-index theorem therefore survives. Adding suitable independent qubits preserves the gap, producing counterexamples for every qubit count of at least three, including strictly positive examples. Repeating independent copies adds their gaps: neither a constant correction nor one growing sublinearly with qubit number can repair the universal Shannon bound.

The replacement uses collision entropy, the negative logarithm of the sum of squared probabilities. The authors expand this sum into products of four amplitudes. Fourier orthogonality removes incompatible terms; taking absolute values bounds the remaining sum by that of the comparison distribution. Since Shannon entropy is at least collision entropy, this yields a valid lower bound for arbitrary affine blocks; conditioning on the block strengthens it. Higher integer powers recover the earlier integer-index theorem. Furthermore, when amplitude magnitudes are constant within each block, the comparison concentrates each block’s entire probability on one outcome. Other phases merely split these masses, which cannot lower any Rényi entropy. The original bound thus holds at every index for this class, including the earlier paper’s two Figure 1 product-state families, without certifying their numerical implementation.

The evidence has separate scopes. The manuscript attributes to a pinned Lean declaration a formal proof only of the signed, full-support Shannon refutation. Written proofs establish the extensions, real-basis reduction, and replacement bounds. Reported finite computations check transforms, bound a specified strictly positive example’s gap between 0.137 and 0.139 nats using rational logarithm estimates, and enumerate 64 phase choices for the three-qubit fixed-support Shannon minimum. They do not search other support matrices. This is a targeted correction to a proposed search bound, not a rejection of the earlier framework: the counterexample settles neither its additivity questions nor its strong-monotonicity questions.
